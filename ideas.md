# Design brief — The Tulip Touch Café

## Design direction
**Editorial Gallery Café** — the user-selected direction. The experience treats the café as a meeting point between contemporary art, coffee culture, and slow conversation. It should feel like an independent creative studio built a hospitality website, not a restaurant template.

## Design movement
A monochrome editorial language inspired by gallery catalogues, photo essays, and luxury cultural publications: confident typographic scale, quiet surfaces, thin rules, asymmetric crops, and restrained motion.

## Core principles
- Let the brand breathe: oversized type and generous negative space.
- Use image, type, and detail labels as composition rather than decoration.
- Keep every claim factual; missing operational details remain configurable placeholders.
- Make the menu feel like a printed insert, but keep filtering obvious and accessible.
- Use one warm off-white surface against deep ink and graphite accents.

## Color philosophy
- Ink `#161616` for the primary canvas and navigation.
- Warm paper `#F1EEE7` for the main reading surface.
- Chalk `#FAF9F6` for contrast sections.
- Graphite `#70716D` for secondary text and metadata.
- Accent `#B8A48B` as a muted coffee-stain beige, used sparingly for active states and small editorial details.

## Layout paradigm
A single long-form page with a compact sticky top navigation, full-bleed opening moment, numbered editorial sections, and staggered two-column grids. Desktop uses a 12-column rhythm and asymmetrical image/text blocks; mobile collapses to a clean linear reading order with touch-safe controls.

## Signature elements
- Vertical section indices like `01 / THE ROOM`.
- Hairline rules and tiny uppercase metadata.
- Oversized serif headlines paired with a precise sans-serif UI.
- Paper-like menu surface on a dark stage.
- Gallery captions that read like exhibition labels.
- A floating contact disk only when contact details are configured; otherwise a clearly labeled configuration placeholder in the contact section.

## Interaction philosophy
Controls should feel tactile and calm. Menu filters update instantly without navigation. Gallery images open in a keyboard-accessible lightbox. All links are semantic and all unavailable operational actions are visibly disabled—not hidden or misleading.

## Animation
Use short fade-up and image-reveal transitions, hover lift on interactive elements, and a compact nav state on scroll. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by disabling non-essential transitions and parallax-like movement.

## Typography system
- Display: `Cormorant Garamond`, high-contrast editorial serif, with Georgia fallback.
- UI/body: `DM Sans`, compact modern sans-serif, with Arial fallback.
- Eyebrow metadata: DM Sans, uppercase, tracked generously.

## Brand essence
Art, coffee, café culture, monochrome interiors, cozy atmosphere, photography-friendly corners, food, desserts, mocktails, coffee, and shakes — conveyed with restraint rather than unsupported superlatives.

## Brand voice
Observant, warm, concise, cultured, and grounded. Use phrases such as “coffee meets art,” “a café shaped by art, coffee and conversation,” and “an atmosphere worth lingering in” without claiming superiority.

## Wordmark / logo
A text-led wordmark paired with a simple two-tulip line emblem. This is a tasteful website treatment, not a claim to be the café’s official logo. The mark uses two broad filled petals and a single stem silhouette so it remains legible at favicon size.

## Signature brand color
Muted coffee-stain beige `#B8A48B`, reserved for small accents so the site remains sophisticated monochrome.

## Asset plan
- Hero: one editorial still-life / interior-inspired atmospheric image, clearly labeled in the UI as an editorial atmosphere image until the café supplies original photography.
- Gallery: data-driven monochrome editorial tiles using the same approved visual family with captions that identify them as replaceable visual studies, never as verified café photos.
- Logo: project-specific flat SVG mark for header and favicon.
